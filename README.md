# NBA Player Contract Value and Acquisition Decision-Support Tool

## Project Overview

The Player Contract Value and Acquisition Decision-Support Tool would help an NBA front office identify players who provide strong performance relative to their contract cost. The tool would compare player production with salary information to identify potential acquisition targets who could strengthen the roster without creating unnecessary salary-cap pressure. The concept could support teams such as the Denver Nuggets as they evaluate roster construction decisions around highly compensated star players.

## Decision-Making Problem

NBA teams must balance player performance with increasingly restrictive financial limits when constructing competitive rosters. Expensive contracts for supporting players can reduce roster flexibility and make future transactions more difficult. The proposed analytics tool would support decisions concerning free-agent targets and potential trades by showing which players generate strong production relative to their financial cost.

## Proposed Analytics Approach

The analysis would combine player performance statistics with contract and salary information. Performance measures could include scoring efficiency and rebounding, along with measures of playmaking contribution. Contract values would allow the organization to compare production against financial commitment. The resulting analysis could identify players whose performance exceeds the level expected from players with similar salaries.

## Use by Decision Makers

Front-office executives could use the tool during roster planning and player acquisition discussions. Coaches and scouts could review identified players to determine whether their playing style fits the team's needs. The analytics would narrow a large pool of possible players into a smaller group that warrants further organizational evaluation.

## Connection to Chapter 7

The project currently represents the creative phase of innovation because the organization has defined the analytics idea and the decision it should support, but it has not tested the proposed enhancement. The next phase would use a prototype to determine whether additional information could improve the usefulness of the concept.

## Prototype Enhancement

The prototype expands the original contract-value concept by adding information about player role and roster fit. The original approach focuses primarily on the relationship between performance and salary. The enhancement would consider whether an undervalued player also fills a meaningful need within the existing roster. A team could use position, expected role, and playing-time information to distinguish financially efficient players who fit the roster from players whose skills duplicate existing personnel.

The additional context could improve decision-making because contract value does not automatically make a player a useful acquisition. Front-office personnel need evidence that a player can contribute within the team's expected rotation and roster structure. The prototype would give decision makers a more practical way to evaluate potential targets.
